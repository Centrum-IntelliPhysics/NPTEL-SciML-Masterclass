Scientific Machine Learning for Computational Mechanics

Physics-informed neural networks and neural operators, built from scratch in PyTorch on three problems you can check by hand: a burn, a bookshelf and a hidden book.

Course introduction from the instructor (13 min).

You build every method yourself, on a problem you can verify

Most tutorials stop once the network trains. These sessions also show you how to check that it is right, and what to do when it is not.

01

One problem per session

Heat in skin, a loaded shelf, a shelf with a hidden book. Each has an exact answer, so every error you see is a real error.

02

You write the code

Every worksheet has TODO cells and self-checks that unit-test the physics, so you know a step is correct before you train.

03

Results you reproduce

Every number on the slides comes from a notebook in your kit. Each notebook generates its own data, so there is nothing to download.

04

When it fails, and why

Checklists you can use at work: why a PINN won’t converge, which operator fits your problem, when a physics loss helps and when it hurts.

Every session runs in four modes

Three sessions, one storyline

From one solution, to all solutions, to solutions you can trust

Each session answers the question the previous one leaves open.

Live problemTea at 70 °C spills on your hand. How deep is the burn after five seconds?

Hands-on W1. Build the skin-burn PINN from scratch, validate it against the exact solution, and turn the temperature field into a burn depth with the Henriques damage integral. Then the whole class sweeps the physics-loss weight together.

Session 1 · 2 hours

Teaching physics to a neural network: PINNs

You will learn

  • Why data-only surrogates fail with sparse sensors
  • Activations and automatic differentiation for PDEs
  • Building a PINN loss: residual, boundary and initial terms, collocation, scaling
  • Inverse problems: an unknown parameter becomes one more trainable weight
  • Failure modes and remedies: loss imbalance, spectral bias, sharp features, causality

Research showcases

  • Locating a hidden crack in aircraft aluminium from ultrasound
  • Estimating a tumour’s growth rate from scans
  • Energy-based PINNs for fracture

Offline practice

  • O1 · Heated metal rod, forward and inverse
  • O2 · PINN failure lab: four broken problems to fix

Live problemAny books, any arrangement: predict how a wall shelf sags, in a millisecond.

Hands-on W2. Generate data from a beam solver, build and train a DeepONet, and test it on shelves it has never seen. Then a design challenge: load the shelf as much as you can and stay under the sag limit.

Session 2 · 2 hours

From one solution to all solutions: neural operators

You will learn

  • Operators: learning the map from input functions to solution functions
  • DeepONet from the universal approximation theorem for operators
  • The trunk learns mode shapes; the branch learns their amplitudes
  • Designing training data and sensors; how much data you need
  • When the vanilla DeepONet fails: NOMAD, MIONet, Latent DeepONet

Live demo

  • A burn-clinic operator that answers 1,000 scenarios at once, and the questions where it quietly fails

Research showcases

  • Brain motion from MRI, age and vibration frequency (multi-input operators)
  • Latent DeepONet for atmospheric flow

Offline practice

  • O3 Traffic-jam fronts (Burgers) · O4 NOMAD · O5 MIONet · O6 Latent DeepONet

Live problemOne book is hidden on the shelf. Where is it, how heavy is it, and can you trust the answer?

Hands-on W3. Train a physics-informed operator with zero labelled data, run a class experiment with deliberately wrong physics, then find your own hidden book from six strain gauges.

Session 3 · 2 hours

Physics-informed operators, inverse problems and hybrid solvers

You will learn

  • Physics-informed DeepONets trained without a single solver run
  • When a physics loss helps, and when wrong physics makes things worse
  • Separable operators for 3D problems
  • Inverse problems through operators, and checking identifiability
  • Discovering hidden physics (DHPO)
  • Coupling operators with numerical solvers: Schwarz, FE–NO, ANCHOR

Live demo

  • Finite differences and a DeepONet solving a cooling fin together

Research showcases

  • Thermal conductivity from 40 temperature sensors
  • Hidden reaction physics, extrapolated in time
  • Finite element–neural operator coupling in elastodynamics

Offline practice

  • O7 Cooling fin, no solver data · O8 Detective with uncertainty · O9 Hybrid solver lab · O10 Hidden physics

Learning outcomes

What you will be able to do

  • Explain how a PINN encodes the PDE, boundary and initial conditions in its loss, and how automatic differentiation computes the residual.
  • Build and train a PINN from scratch in PyTorch and validate it against an analytical solution.
  • Distinguish solving one PDE instance from learning an operator that generalises across loads, boundary conditions and sources.
  • Implement a DeepONet, design its training data, and measure its accuracy with the relative L2 error.
  • Decide when a physics loss improves training and when it introduces errors, and choose an architecture to match.
  • Solve an inverse problem through a trained operator and judge whether the problem is identifiable.
  • Couple a neural operator with a classical solver by domain decomposition.
  • Name the open challenges in scientific machine learning and pick the right tool for your own problem.

Questions

Frequently asked

Who is this masterclass for?

Graduate students, researchers and engineers in mechanical, civil, aerospace, chemical and biomedical engineering, or in applied mathematics, who simulate physical systems and want to use machine learning with them. If you can write a Python function and a for-loop, you can follow the worksheets.

Do I need a GPU or a powerful laptop?

No. Every worksheet runs on the free Colab CPU. The longest training cell in the live worksheets takes about two to three minutes on a Colab CPU, and under a minute on a free T4 GPU.

Which framework do the worksheets use?

PyTorch only. Session 3 also uses torch.func for derivatives, which is part of PyTorch 2.0 and later. You can run the notebooks in a local Jupyter installation as well.

What if I fall behind during a hands-on block?

Each TODO has a folded hint and a self-check that tells you whether your code is right. The offline worksheets let you practise each idea again at your own pace.

Are the sessions recorded?

Yes, NPTEL records the live sessions. See the NPTEL registration page for how recordings are made available.

How do fees and certificates work?

Registration, fees and certificates are handled by NPTEL. Please see the registration page.
